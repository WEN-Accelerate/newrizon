# Benchmark Review

What we studied before designing the foundation's website, and what we're taking (or deliberately rejecting) from each.

## 1. Reference site — D2CX by Inc42 (d2cx.co)

A conversion-focused cohort/community landing page for India's D2C founders.

**What works (borrow):**
- **"Who should join" audience segmentation** — visitors self-identify (founder / retail brand / aspiring operator) and each segment gets its own value pitch. We will do the same with *students / educators / parents / donors / partners*.
- **Outcome-first copy** — "10X your brand in 12 weeks." Every program states a concrete outcome, not a vague mission.
- **Proof stacked early** — numbers bar (350+ founders, NPS 88), mentor faces, testimonials above the fold.
- **Single relentless CTA** — one primary action per page, sticky on scroll.
- **Dark, bold, modern aesthetic** — feels like a startup, not an institution.

**What we reject:**
- It is a single long landing page; a foundation needs a true multipage information architecture (programs, impact reporting, governance, media).
- Sales-page urgency (deadlines, scarcity) is wrong for a philanthropic brand — we keep the clarity, drop the pressure.

## 2. Astronaut Scholarship Foundation (astronautscholarship.org)

Created by the Mercury 7 astronauts. Classic nonprofit IA: Programs / Events / Scholars / Innovators Gala.

- **Borrow:** the *named-program* structure (Astronaut Scholarship, Michael Collins Professional Development Program) — programs as products with their own identity; the annual flagship event (Innovators Gala) as a recurring anchor; a public, celebrated **scholar alumni directory**.
- **Reject:** conventional, template-like visual design. Nothing about the site *feels* like space.

## 3. Kalpana Chawla Scholars (kalpanachawlascholars.org)

Legacy-driven: the astronaut's personal story is the emotional core; scholarship funds Indian women into the International Space University.

- **Borrow:** the founder-story-as-foundation-story pattern; a tightly defined flagship scholarship with a clear beneficiary profile.
- **Reject:** thin content beyond the story; little for a visitor to *do*.

## 4. Obama Foundation (obama.org) — 2025 Webby winner

- **Borrow:** split-screen storytelling (fixed narrative panel + scrolling imagery), hero video, editorial confidence, program brands (Leaders, Scholars, My Brother's Keeper) under one masterbrand.
- **Note:** their scale of content is enormous; we adopt the patterns, not the page count.

## 5. The Planetary Society (planetary.org)

- **Borrow:** membership as the core relationship (not just donations); "become a member / advocate" framing; educational content engine that earns repeat visits.

## 6. Club for the Future — Blue Origin (clubforfuture.org)

- **Borrow:** *participatory* mechanics — "Send a postcard to space" is a physical, shareable, zero-cost action any child can take. This is the single best engagement idea in the category and we will build our own version.

## 7. Space Kidz India (spacekidzindia.in)

- **Borrow:** hands-on credibility — students actually build and launch things (ShakthiSAT). Programs framed as *missions* with real hardware outcomes.

## Category gap = our opening

Every astronaut/space foundation site is either (a) an emotional legacy page with little to do, or (b) a conventional nonprofit template that doesn't feel like space at all. **Nobody has built the site itself as a space mission.** That's the out-of-the-box position we take — detailed in `02-concept.md`.
